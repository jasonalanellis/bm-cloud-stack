Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is considered "normal wear and tear" versus damage?

A: Normal wear and tear includes minor scuffs, faded paint, or slightly worn carpet in high-traffic areas. Damage goes beyond this. Examples include large holes in walls, broken fixtures, or significant stains. Your lease often defines these terms.

Q: Can my landlord charge me for cleaning if I cleaned the property myself?

A: Yes, if the landlord deems your cleaning insufficient to meet the lease's standards. Many leases specify a professional-level of cleanliness. Documentation of your efforts is vital. However, a landlord cannot charge for cleaning beyond restoring the property to its pre-move-in condition, accounting for normal wear.

Q: How long does a landlord have to return my security deposit in San Diego?

A: In California, landlords have 21 calendar days after you move out to return your security deposit. They must provide an itemized statement for any deductions. This timeframe is a legal requirement.

Q: What should I do if I disagree with my landlord's deductions?

A: First, send a written letter disputing the deductions. Provide your documentation (photos, receipts). If this doesn't resolve it, you can file a claim in small claims court. Consult local tenant-landlord resources for specific guidance in San Diego.
