Protecting Delicate Surfaces: Stone and Metal Care

San Diego’s high-end homes often feature premium natural stone surfaces, such as travertine, marble, slate, and granite. While these materials add undeniable luxury and aesthetic appeal, they are highly sensitive to improper cleaning agents.

Acidic formulas will etch travertine and strip protective sealants. Always use pH-neutral chemistry for these delicate materials. This preserves the natural luster of the stone floor.

When untrained cleaners use acidic bathroom descalers, vinegar, or harsh citrus-based solutions on natural stone, they cause irreversible chemical burns. These burns appear as dull, white spots or cloudy patches that can only be repaired through costly professional diamond grinding and polishing. Our specialists are trained to identify the exact mineral composition of every surface before selecting a cleaning agent. By applying pH-neutral, stone-safe surfactants, we lift dirt and oils from the porous stone matrix while maintaining the integrity of the protective sealants, ensuring the stone looks radiant under open-house lighting.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between a standard house cleaning and a real estate deep clean?

A standard house cleaning focuses on surface-level maintenance, such as dusting visible surfaces, vacuuming high-traffic areas, and basic steam-clean of bathrooms and kitchens. A real estate deep clean is an exhaustive, detail-oriented restoration process. It targets areas that are typically overlooked during routine cleanings, such as the interiors of all appliances, inside cabinets and drawers, deep window tracks, baseboards, light fixtures, and outlet covers. The goal is to return the home to a "like-new" state, eliminating any trace of the previous occupant so potential buyers can easily envision themselves living in the space.

Why shouldn't we use vinegar or multi-purpose cleaners on travertine and marble floors?

Many people believe vinegar is a safe, natural cleaner, but it is highly acidic. Travertine, marble, and limestone are composed of calcium carbonate, which chemically reacts with acids. When an acidic cleaner touches these stones, it dissolves the surface minerals, leaving dull etch marks. Multi-purpose cleaners often have a high pH (alkaline) or low pH (acidic) and can strip away the protective sealants that prevent stains from penetrating the porous stone. To protect your investment and maintain the stone's natural luster, you must always use pH-neutral, stone-safe formulas.

How does the coastal San Diego climate affect the cleaning process?

San Diego's coastal climate introduces salt spray, high humidity, and marine layers. This environment creates a sticky, saline residue on windows, exterior siding, and outdoor metal fixtures. This residue acts like glue, trapping dust, sand, and pollen. Coastal properties require specialized cleaning techniques, particularly for exterior windows and metal fixtures, to strip away the salt layer and prevent permanent mineral etching and corrosion.

Do you clean the inside of appliances and cabinets during a real estate clean?

Yes, absolutely. Buyers routinely inspect storage capacity and appliance cleanliness during home tours. Finding a dirty oven, a dusty pantry shelf, or a stained cabinet interior can negatively impact their perception of the property's overall maintenance. Our real estate cleaning services always include thorough interior and exterior detailing of all built-in appliances, kitchen cabinets, bathroom vanities, and linen closets.

How far in advance of listing photos should the cleaning be scheduled?

We highly recommend scheduling the deep clean 1 to 2 days before your professional photography session. This ensures the home is in absolute peak condition for the camera. It also allows time for any staging furniture to be arranged on perfectly clean floors, maximizing the visual impact of your online listing.
